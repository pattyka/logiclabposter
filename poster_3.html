What the market said, and what is not yet proven

What the market said

Demand was tested against behaviour, not opinion: a landing page presented LogicLab Kids as a finished product, with a live paid sign-up at HUF 1,890 / month, and went through KRÉTA, the national school platform, to every parent at my school.

Twenty-family beta

Twenty families from those sign-ups used an early build. Their reports converged: the children thought more independently, reached for TikTok less, and offered their own opinions more often.

To be exact: parent-reported and uncontrolled. It shows families want this and notice a change. It does not show the app causes it.

What is not yet proven

Whether the app causes the change, and whether the reward sustains engagement without eroding the intrinsic motivation it is meant to build. The next phase answers both.

Cohort30 children aged 7–11 plus a matched control group; pre/post design.
InstrumentsRaven Progressive Matrices (curriculum-independent reasoning); WPPSI-IV subtests indexing executive function.
In-apptutor turns needed to reach a sound conclusion, tracked over time; parent and teacher questionnaire.
Rewardsalience stepped down over the study: do engagement and reasoning persist as the extrinsic reward fades?

We don’t ban AI, we prepare children for it.

A generative AI is neither hero nor villain in a child’s education; it is whatever its design makes it: it offloads the child’s thinking when built to answer and provokes it when built to ask. The application exists and runs, the demand is validated rather than assumed, and the limits are named: a controlled trial is next, the overjustification risk is monitored, not solved, and the privacy audit precedes public release.
