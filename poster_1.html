LogicLab Kids · EUCYS

When AI thinks, the child’s mind does less of it

I was on a tram when a ten-year-old told his father that ChatGPT had done his homework, and the father just chuckled, but something didnt sit right with me. Turns out this is one of the most detramental issues in the modern age.

Cognitive Debt

MIT did a study where they tested AIs effect on our brains. They found that people using chat gpt to write essays showcased up to 47% lower brain activity.

The Critical Window

Executive function develops fastest between ages 7 and 11; like a muscle, if it isn’t worked then, it’s significantly harder to build later. And in the US, half of children own a smartphone by 11.

The Collision

Children aged 8–12 spend 5 h 33 min a day on entertainment screens, teens 8 h 39 min, up 17% in two years. The scarce resource is attention, not content.

What I set out to build and test

Pedagogical. Make reasoning learnable the way arithmetic is: by practice, not exposition. The child builds an argument, finds the broken step, weighs evidence. And one which the child actually wants to open.

Technical. A native app where for each completed lesson the child gets screentime which the parent sets up once.

Research. Test against a control whether reasoning measurably improves, and whether the reward erodes the motivation it is meant to build; publish whatever the result.

The hypothesis: make thinking the price of screen time

Premack (1959): a higher-probability behaviour can reinforce a lower-probability one. Screen entertainment is a child’s highest-probability behaviour and reasoning practice among the lowest, so finishing a lesson earns minutes that unlock the apps the parent chose, and iOS, not the app, holds the lock. The known objection, the overjustification effect, is measured in the planned study.
